# Data source registry, the Radar's sources and their licences

> **The admission rule** (decided on 2026-07-22): a source gets in only if its licence permits
> reuse on a commercial site **in writing**. This site sells (Services, Booking). Ambiguous
> means out, with no convenient interpretations. Attribution is not a courtesy: the licences
> below grant the use **in exchange** for the citation. This file is the compliance record,
> versioned and dated.
>
> Every verdict was reached by reading the source's **actual licence page** (not the API docs,
> not a blog post, not from memory). Verdicts are dated per entry: the oldest are from
> 2026-07-22, when the rule was set, and later rounds carry their own date in the row.

## Approved but not wired

One, as of 2026-07-25: **ACSC** (Australia), whose licence clears but whose wiring is blocked
by a reachability doubt (detail below). Every other source that cleared the licence gate is in
use above: those without a feed enter as a **point with no bulletins** (the EU style) rather
than sitting on the bench, since a verified licence is the only condition of entry.

### Why three of the sources in use have no feed

| Source | What was verified |
|---|---|
| **NCSC NZ** | `/rss/` and `/newsroom/rss/` answer **200 with a "Page not found" page** (a soft 404). There is not a single `application/rss+xml` in the source: their *Subscribe* is a mailing list. There is a JSON-LD with 89 alerts (url and name) but **no dates**, and the Radar sorts by date and promises "today's bulletins". It would admit a list that cannot keep that promise |
| **International AI Safety Report** | No `rss` in the source (verified by Marco on 2026-07-24) |
| **White House** | The `/presidential-actions/feed/` feed **exists and is valid** (200, `application/rss+xml`, zero redirects), but it carries *all* presidential actions. Measured on 2026-07-24: **0 items out of 30** concerned AI (June's executive order was already outside the window). A keyword filter would feed an empty point 95% of the time, so no freshness claim is better |

### ACSC (Australia), approved but not wired

The licence is fine: **CC BY 4.0**, *"All material presented on this website is provided under
a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence"*, excluding the Coat of Arms, the
ACSC logo and third-party material
([cyber.gov.au/acsc/copyright](https://cyber.gov.au/acsc/copyright), updated 2026-01-13).

There is also a reachability doubt, since `cyber.gov.au` does not answer from the development

## How to look for a new source (the criterion, not the list)

This emerged on 2026-07-25 after a fruitless round across nine countries: **you do not look for
a CERT, you look for a jurisdiction**. Every approved source above sits in a country with a
national open government policy: the British OGL, the French Licence Ouverte, the Dutch CC0,
the New Zealand and Australian CC BY. None got in because it was a CERT. Each got in because
that government had already decided, by law, that its content can be reused.

The practical corollary: team directories (FIRST, various indexes) are for **enumerating**, not
for selecting. Working through them costs one verification each and produces almost nothing but
no's. The round on 25 July finished 0 for 9. Starting instead from the list of countries with an
open public licence, and only then looking for the CERT, inverts the ratio between checks and
results.

## How to add a source

1. Find the **real licence or terms page** and read it (do not trust searches or summaries).
2. Does the licence permit commercial use in writing? A no or an ambiguity means it does not get in.
3. Add the entry here (with quotes and links) **and** to `astro-project/src/data/radar-fonti.js`
   (with `hostsAmmessi`: feed links are discarded if they point outside the source's domains).
4. The `radar.test.mjs` test ("every source in the registry has a written licence") acts as the
   guard: a source without `licenza.nome`/`licenza.url` does not pass CI.
5. **Verify the feed by running it, not by reading it**:
   `curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code} %{redirect_url}"`. It needs a 200 **with no redirect**
   (`scarica()` uses `redirect: 'manual'`) and a body that really is XML. A 200 serving HTML is
   a soft 404 and produces a source that is alive and empty.

**Sources that are not a stream.** If the source is a versioned taxonomy rather than a feed (the
MITRE ATLAS case), the data gets committed: a script in `engine/` downloads it and writes a
module into `src/data/`, and the registry entry uses `itemsStatici` instead of `feeds`. The
committed items pass through the **same** `hostsAmmessi` barrier as the feed ones, and two tests
verify it, one on the generated data and one on the barrier.
