title: "Claims handled without human intervention: the Singapore case"
problem: "The insurance industry faces a structural problem in claims handling: manual processes, long turnaround times, and rising operational costs are making traditional models increasingly unsustainable. According to KPMG, insurers are shifting from isolated pilot projects to structured transformation across the entire value chain, from underwriting to distribution. The core issue is that claims, historically the point of greatest friction with customers, still require too many manual checks before a payout can be made."
approach: "The response has been end-to-end claims automation through AI systems capable of assessing, deciding, and executing with no human involvement in most cases. In Singapore, a mature insurance market, a touchless processing model was applied: claims are automatically assessed by algorithms that analyze data and documentation, reserving human intervention only for cases requiring further checks. The system was designed to handle the full cycle, from intake to settlement."
result: "The result, documented by KPMG for the Singapore market, is that 98% of claims are now auto-assessed, and around 60% are processed and paid within 24 hours with no human intervention at all. This marks a sharp break from traditional claims handling, which in many markets still relies on manual processes and sequential checks spanning several days."
lesson: "The lesson is that claims automation works when it is designed as a complete process rather than a single isolated feature: the value comes from linking intake, validation, decision, and execution into one automated chain. At the same time, the more autonomy a system gains, the greater the need for traceability and proportional oversight, since customer trust depends on the ability to explain any disputed outcome."
